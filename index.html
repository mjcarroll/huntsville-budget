Huntsville City Schools

The Huntsville City Board of Education is a legally separate state agency, not a City department — but the City funds a meaningful share of it (see the "Taxes" and "Intergovernmental Assistance" pass-throughs in the Budget Flow view) and issues bonds on its behalf for capital projects. Figures below come from HCS's own audited financial statements, FY2019–FY2024, filed separately from the City's ACFR.

Total Revenue vs. Expenses

Government-wide (Statement of Activities) totals, FY2019–FY2024.

Per-Pupil Spending

Total revenue and expense divided by enrollment. Enrollment is essentially flat over this period, so nearly all of the growth here is real cost-per-student growth, not a bigger student body.

Revenue Mix

Ad valorem (property) tax revenue more than doubled starting FY2022, even as enrollment stayed flat.

Expense Mix

By function, per each year's Management's Discussion & Analysis.

Net Position

Net position roughly doubled FY2021–FY2024, but a large share of that is an actuarial artifact of the OPEB liability swing below, not new operating strength.

Net Pension & OPEB Liability

The OPEB liability collapses seen in FY2020 and again FY2023 aren't benefit cuts — they're the State's PEEHIP retiree health plan re-rating its discount-rate assumption (the "Single Equivalent Interest Rate"), which swings the reported liability by tens of millions with no change in HCS's actual cash obligations.

Board-Issued Debt

Bonds, warrants, and leases on HCS's own balance sheet — steadily amortizing down, with no new HCS-issued debt since FY2019.

City-Issued Bonds for HCS Capital Projects

Serviced from HCS's share of local property tax but issued by the City on HCS's behalf (par value; only years with a disclosed balance shown — refunding activity makes this non-monotonic).

Governmental Fund Balance

Combined ending balance across all governmental funds, FY2019–FY2024. The FY2023 jump reflects a new bond issuance funding the Central Office/Career Tech construction program (see Capital Projects Fund).

General Fund Balance Composition

GASB fund-balance classification. FY2021 fund-level detail wasn't available in the audits reviewed, so the line interpolates across that gap — FY2020, FY2022–FY2024 are real data points.

All Other Governmental Funds

Special Revenue, Capital Projects, Debt Service, and Other funds combined.

Student Enrollment

Average daily attendance count, FY2006–FY2024 (excludes Pre-K). Essentially flat for two decades, even as the City of Huntsville's population grew from 180,105 (2010 census) to 215,006 (2020 census).

Audit & Governance Notes

  • The FY2019–FY2020 audits flagged excessive turnover and understaffing in the Finance Department as a significant deficiency, requiring outside consulting help to keep the department running.
  • The FY2020–FY2023 audits repeatedly cited timecards not approved before payroll across multiple federal programs (Special Education, ESSER, Title I, Child Nutrition); the finding was marked corrected in the FY2024 audit.
  • The audit firm changed from Mercer & Associates, P.C. (FY2019–FY2023) to Aldridge, Borden and Company, P.C. (FY2024).

The Budget Flow view's "Bond Proceeds & Transfers (net)" / "Added to Fund Balance" nodes stand in for each fund's "Other Financing Sources (Uses)" (debt issuance, bond premiums, transfers between funds), netted per fund so revenue in equals expenditure out. The multi-year trend views (Revenue & Expenditures, Fund Balance, Debt & Capital, Growth) use the ACFR's own 10-year statistical schedules, which reflect routine prior-period reclassifications and can differ slightly from the year-by-year figures in the Budget Flow view.

Schools (HCS) source: Huntsville City Board of Education audited financial statements, FY2019–FY2024, from the HCS Finance Department's audits page.
